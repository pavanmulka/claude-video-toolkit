# Short-video playbook (what's working now)

Read before grooming any idea. **One pattern = one line**: pattern, *why*, tool, evidence `[views: record ids]` (short ids
work: `./vtk kb show lmxpbp`) or `[source, date]`. A source that only confirms a line raises its evidence; new lines only
for new patterns; contradicted lines go to Fading, then `PLAYBOOK_ARCHIVE.md`; no dated blocks; budget 150 lines / 16 KB
(`./vtk kb`), over it merge and archive the weakest; recent evidence outweighs old. Base: the library (`./vtk kb stats`),
research of 2026-09-24, Sept 2026 baseline (TikTok Creative Starter Pack + Codes, Meta Reels guide, Socialinsider, OpusClip, Buffer).

## Hooks (first 1–2 s)
- **Proof on frame 1, key message inside 3 s, outcome first**: "Your flight's gate in 2 taps". *Outcome/product-first
  hooks got ~2x the views of the weakest type; keep the key element centred and still for 1 s or more.* Tool: `kinetic`
  text, full size on frame 0. [[OpusClip, 34,635 TikToks, Apr 2026](https://www.opus.pro/blog/tiktok-hooks-that-go-viral-2026);
  [JAMS eye-tracking, Mar 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-025-01137-x)]
- **Script the hook as a curiosity loop in 3 beats**: **context lean** (say the topic plainly so the right viewers
  self-select; pull them in with a benefit, pain, metaphor or surprising fact) → **scroll-stop interjection** (one short
  line with a contrast word: "But…", "However…") → **contrarian snapback** (same topic, the opposite direction). The bigger
  the snap, the stronger the hook, but the payoff must be on screen or viewers churn (your brand's versions: its MARKETING.md). [828K: lmxpbp]
- **Check every hook for the four killers**. A hook's one job is to make the right viewer opt in: topic clarity +
  on-target curiosity. **Delay:** the topic lands in the first 1–2 s (cut fluff; "you won't believe this" says nothing).
  **Confusion:** fewer, simpler words (5th grade or under: say what "profit" means instead of the word; MrBeast's Shorts
  read at ~1st grade; test: would a 10-year-old who barely speaks English get it?), active voice; if it reads two ways,
  rewrite. **Irrelevance:**
  you/your (not I/me/we) and a need-to-have pain, not a nice-to-know topic. **Disinterest:** contrast A (what they believe
  or do now) vs B (the better way); stated names both, implied names only B. Hooks are 2–3 lines: clarity, then contrast.
  [989K: 2bypp; 4.9M: as7abw]
- **Visual hook first**: 3–5 bold words that set the topic on frame 1, over a visual with *just enough* motion (the 3D
  phone settle, a tap). Text + motion + voice together beat voice alone. Design the first frame before the words, like a
  long-form thumbnail: it should work as a title + thumbnail and make sense muted. [828K: lmxpbp; 4.9M: as7abw]
- **Series framing**: one fixed first-frame layout per series: the place or thing people already know (the restaurant,
  not her face) + the item + "$1 burrito" dead centre, the same every episode, so viewers recognise it and binge the
  playlist. [4.9M: as7abw]
- **Write 10 hooks, test the best**: "how would [big creator] say this?" for ~10 big creators, pick favourites, twist
  them into your own, test 2–3 on non-followers (Instagram Trial Reels) and keep the best viewed-vs-swiped.
  [4.9M: as7abw; [Meta, Dec 2024](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/)]
- **Lead with the benefit or pain, then the thing** ("If you want better sleep, you need magnesium"). **Borrow
  familiarity** (airplane mode, Apple's privacy label, the Notes app, the junk drawer). **First value within ~4 s**;
  staccato hook sentences, longer ones later. [828K: lmxpbp]
- **Recognisable moment**: "When the group chat asks who still owes money". *Viewers see themselves in it.* Tool:
  `problem-payoff` / `pov` templates. [baseline]
- **Ask the question the last line answers, and make it the title**: "Can you cook faster than fast food?", "How many
  flavors can you get with $1?", "What's faster: X or Y?". Said from 0.0 s (43 of 61 narrated hits say the title in the
  first 3 s). Variants: "I'm doing X with $0", a label ("One dollar boba."), "My mom thinks X. But what she doesn't
  know…" (a secret the viewer shares). *The viewer knows the game in 1–2 s and must stay for the answer.* [top 80, up to 185M: jennyhoyos]
- Also works: a specific number, a direct command, a contrarian line (only if the video proves it). **Avoid** "Hi
  everyone", "Okay so…", "Have you ever wondered…", and opening on the app name or logo. [baseline; a number in 32% of top hooks: jennyhoyos]
- Hook text: 6–10 words, inside the 3:4 profile-grid crop, and the VO says it too. [baseline; jennyhoyos]

## Narration & voice
- **Talk from frame 1 and never stop**: speech at 0.04 s, ~11 words in the first 3 s, ~3.6 words/s, under 1 pause per
  10 s; ~140 words for a ~39 s story. Tool: ElevenLabs VO + `max_pause: 0.35`. [medians of the top 80: jennyhoyos]
- **Hook + foreshadow in ≤ 3 s**: line 1 the premise, line 2 promises the ending ("…and I'm going to surprise someone
  with $100 at the end"); a personal why + irony makes people care ("my kitchen's broken, so I cook to raise money").
  Then a transition that keeps the pace ("so I cooked it illegally"), never "let's get started". Write the hook and the
  last line (a blank for the reaction) before filming. [4.9M: as7abw]
- **Chain the story**: line 2 = who + the stake ("my grandma spends way too much on boba"), then the plan ("so I'm
  gonna…"), then steps joined by so / but / therefore (in ~80% of her scripts), a sceptic to prove wrong, and a **re-hook
  question at ~70%** ("But is it better than her favorite café? Let's find out."). The promised end arrives with a twist
  (mom drops the $5 gift). [jennyhoyos; as7abw]
- **The last words are the payoff, no outro**: 91% of her hits end mid-speech ("…for a dollar I got water."); trimming
  one extra second at the end took a Short from 83% to 88% retention and it took off. Exact numbers are the proof and
  the punchline ("81 cents", "$248"; 3.3 per 100 words). [jennyhoyos; as7abw]
- **AI voice**: label realistic AI audio on TikTok and Meta; most viewers accept it when disclosed, but AI-made ads
  underperform, so keep the script human-written, the voice natural and brisk, the mix warm.
  [[IAB, Jan 2026](https://www.iab.com/insights/the-ai-gap-widens/); [HBR, Sept 2026](https://hbr.org/2026/09/research-ai-generated-ads-perform-worse-than-human-made-ones-even-when-customers-cant-tell-them-apart)]

## Formats that work for faceless apps
- **Hook + demo**: result first, then 3–5 taps; proof at 1x speed, typing sped up 2–3x.
- **POV / problem → payoff**: chaos cuts about 1 s each, then a hard cut to calm product proof.
- **Numbered list**: 3 items of 4–5 s each, ending on a question to the viewer.
- **Before/after split with timers** (or a **"What's faster: X or Y?" race**, 107M: jennyhoyos), **group-chat skit**,
  **"things I wish I knew"** (the app appears in one tip only), **rating X**, **6-slide story**, **reply to a comment**
  (the platform's native reply sticker). Templates: `./vtk new --list`. [baseline]
- **Expectation fake-out** (trend, Sept 2026): set up something everyone waits for ("I can't believe this plane flew
  that close ⚠️", a real jet roar swelling for ~5 s) and, at the sound's peak, deliver the message instead (a paper
  plane: "Buy a Toyota") while the roar keeps going. No story needed. Original 24.5M; brands reused it as-is (a car
  dealer, a smoothie shop: 1.9–2.4M). [ig breakdown + sound map: dcy8bl]

## Structure & pacing
- **Length and cut per platform** (one spec, `variants:`): TikTok 15–30 s (brand engagement; creators 10–20 s, dense);
  Reels 30–60 s for reach, very visual with captions on every line, made to share; Shorts a slower story (her best: 34 s;
  under 30 s needs 100%+ retention); single-feature demos 20–35 s. The same video got 1M views on one platform and 1K
  on another. [baseline; 4.9M: as7abw, 2023]
- **A visible mechanism toward the promised end**: "3 things we need" with the list on screen, MrBeast's closing circle,
  a tally, a checklist. Viewers know what's at the end and how far they are. [4.9M: as7abw; frame sheets: jennyhoyos]
- **One focal point per shot, a new picture every 1.5–3 s** (3–6 per 10 s). *Cuts focus attention (peak 0.7 s after the
  cut); visual complexity costs 26% of it; more cuts help only up to a point; viewers leave on static visuals and slow
  intros.* Never freeze: push in slowly. Punch-ins of 10–15% on alternate shots; `autozoom` on each tap; proof moments
  1.5–2.5 s at 1x. [[JAMS, Mar 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11747-025-01137-x); [Internet Research, Feb 2026](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/intr-07-2023-0521/full/html); baseline]
- **Loop endings pay**: Shorts replays count as views (since March 2025). End on a frame that matches the start
  (`output.loop`); no fade to black; end card at most 1–1.5 s. Reveal-first videos open on the result ("Tada.") and end
  on the countdown to it ("3, 2…"). [YouTube, Mar 2025; baseline; 65M: jennyhoyos]

## Text, captions & fonts
- **Captions are expected** (about half of US viewers use them often): max 2 lines, 1.5–3 s per block; keep text out of
  the top 10–14% and the bottom 20–35%. `captions: {skip: [hook]}` when the hook text already shows the words.
  [[XR, May 2026](https://www.xr.global/press-releases/captions-are-no-longer-optional-u-s-audiences-now-expect-them-everywhere-they-watch-video)]
- Clean look (`clean` / `reveal`, CLAUDE.md typography): sentence case, soft shadow + thin outline, keyword in the brand
  colour, 1–3 words per chunk (3–5 for tutorials), ~60–65% down; ALL CAPS only for hooks; one emoji max per element. [baseline]
- **Creator-native text**: TikTok Sans SemiBold, lowercase, white with a thick black outline, 1–4 words at the centre
  (≈ our `word_highlight` captions in lowercase); a second speaker in yellow under a small "mom:" label; running counts
  in an outlined pill (": 4", "$1.07"); an emoji checklist that ticks off steps; red arrows on the key object. Her
  2025+ captions got smaller and lighter, like the clean look above. [frame sheets: jennyhoyos]

## Motion & sound
- Baseline rules live in CLAUDE.md (standing editing rules, "What works now"): hard cuts, 2–3 whips at section changes,
  one flash on the reveal, one phone move, marks / lifts on proof only; effects on 3–5 beats, original audio only. [baseline]
- **Let the sound make the promise, the picture break it**: the audio sets the expectation (a jet roar getting louder
  and brighter, a ringtone, hold music) and the twist lands on its peak. Trends often live in the sound: both fake-out
  versions used the same flyover recording. Read every reference's sound map. [dcy8bl]

## Product reveal, CTA & offers
- The product appears after the problem lands. Show real UI (no mock-ups pretending to be the app). [baseline]
- Soft CTA; a question to viewers drives comments. For ads, CTA end cards lifted recall. A comment-keyword freebie
  ("Comment 'Formats' and we'll DM it") drew one comment per four likes; it needs an auto-DM tool and a real freebie.
  [TikTok data, baseline; dcy8bl]
- **Privacy apps: prove the architecture, use the label as backup.** ~42% of labelled apps claim "Data Not Collected" and
  Apple doesn't verify labels; privacy TikToks get fact-checked in public. Show no account, no company servers, works in
  airplane mode. [[arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.02658); [PoPETs 2024](https://petsymposium.org/popets/2024/popets-2024-0111.php);
  [TechCrunch on Stardust, 2022](https://techcrunch.com/2022/06/27/stardust-period-tracker-phone-number/)]
- **FOMO only with a real limit, after the why.** Fake scarcity is deceptive; "free" pulls far harder than cheap; buyers
  want the reason first. [[FTC, 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/09/ftc-report-shows-rise-sophisticated-dark-patterns-designed-trick-trap-consumers);
  [Marketing Science, 2007](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.1060.0254); [TikTok Next 2026](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/en/next)]

## Ideas & publishing
- **Idea filter** (~1 in 100 gets made): do we want to make it, can we film it, is the hook good, is there a mechanism,
  will people rewatch and share it; the best ideas come from real-life moments (grandma got free detergent by
  complaining → a video). [4.9M: as7abw]
- **Benchmarks** (her Shorts, 2023): viewed-vs-swiped averages ~70%, hers 85%; retention of 90%+ at ~34 s is her floor
  to blow up. Shares help (a 20% share-to-view rate went with 92% viewed); retention isn't everything (a 70%-retention
  Short still reached 10M on returning viewers). [4.9M: as7abw]
- **Repost winners** a few months later with a new hook or cover: Cucumber Hack got 37M twice, What's Faster 107M then 35M. [jennyhoyos]
- **Volume doesn't replace the scripted story**: posting daily early on taught her from every retention graph, but since
  Nov 2025 she posts ~13 Shorts a day at a median of 155–460K views; her scripted 2022–25 run had a median of 1.5M. [jennyhoyos; as7abw]
- **Plan the week 3-2-1**: 3 topic buckets × 2 posts + 1 "chaos agent" test (7 a week). One broad bucket for reach, two
  narrow ones for the buyer's pains (more trust per view); after 2 weeks kill a bucket whose 4 videos flopped. [92K: 1ilmgc]
- **Own a lane (trust beats follower count)**: stack white space on topic, audience slice, delivery, format and look;
  borrow formats from distant niches instead of cloning the leader; hold back case-study proof while small. [92K: 1ilmgc]

## Fading / avoid
- The baseline list lives in CLAUDE.md ("Fading"): Hormozi neon captions, constant caps, glitch or flash on every cut,
  logo openers, over-polished ads, watermarks, near-identical templated uploads, stock AI voices. [baseline]
- Engagement-bait clips ("SUBSCRIBE! U MAY NOT SEE ME AGAIN", 53M) still pull creator views but read as spam for a brand. [jennyhoyos]
